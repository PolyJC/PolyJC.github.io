01 / RESEARCH

From molecular architecture
to collective behavior.

My doctoral work in polymer self-assembly and current work in liquid-crystalline polymers connect through a common question: how does organization inside a material determine what it can do?

01

ARCHITECTURE & CONNECTIVITY

Self-assembled polymer nanostructures

I have developed randomly linked networks, star and branched copolymers, and linear multiblocks that form disordered cocontinuous structures. This work connects molecular architecture with phase connectivity and interconnected porous materials.

Explore a representative paper ↗
02

LIGHT & MOLECULAR ORDER

Liquid-crystalline polymers

In the Page group, I investigate optical and thermal processing of liquid-crystalline polymers. My published work on bulk photoalignment uses in situ monitoring to connect light exposure with the development of optical anisotropy.

Read the photoalignment work ↗
03

STRUCTURE & RESPONSE

Responsive soft materials

My broader research interests connect phase organization and processing history with mechanical response, actuation, and transport. I am interested in how local order and connectivity can produce useful behavior at the scale of a whole material.

04 / TEACHING & MENTORING

Observe. Explain.
Test the idea.

I help students connect hands-on observations with physical reasoning—and develop the confidence to ask their next question.

My experience includes teaching a first-year physics laboratory at Inha University, mentoring polymer researchers at CU Boulder and UT Austin, and co-leading graduate mentoring activities in the Polymer Science and Engineering program at UMass Amherst.

My approach combines practical demonstrations, explicit discussion of controls, and increasing independence. Clear expectations and accessible guidance help students take ownership of their work.
